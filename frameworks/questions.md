# Standing questions (from the Gunnersbury working session)
1. How good is this property?
2. What is it really worth?
3. How healthy is the tenant?
4. Is it worth investing in, and at what price?
5. Where did each historical price/figure come from, and is it verified?
6. What happens if the building empties, and what else could it be used for?
7. Which unconventional, asymmetric data points bear on value?
8. Is there any chance of capital growth from planning?
9. How has rent in the area moved over the last decade?
10. Where did any "cancelled"/"proposed" scheme claim come from? Read the primary source.
11. What could a buyer legally and economically build on the site?
12. Which nearby occupiers face lease expiries, need to expand, or are short of power, and could use this building?
13. Do neighbouring offices disappearing mean less competing supply, or vanishing demand?
14. What would a buyer planning a different use actually bid, after build costs, finance, planning charges and profit?
15. Apply the hard-floor (Fukuoka Dome) way of thinking: what is certain or physical?
16. What asymmetric angles have we not yet used?
17. What is the seller's motive, the macro picture, and what do LinkedIn/job data say about the tenant?
18. Why is the seller selling?
19. If institutions aren't buying this asset type, does the thesis die?
20. If the seller is selling over carbon pathways, how does that affect us?
21. What would it cost to bring the energy rating up?
22. Is carbon-pathway alignment what institutions now look at?
23. Is there any area, income source, regeneration route, or micro/macro data we've missed?
24. If we buy at a given price, how do we protect the downside? Is there a value play or genuine extra income?
